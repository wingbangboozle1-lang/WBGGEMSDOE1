Why did GEMSDOE32 B2 get the highest supplied score (0.2778)? — PhD-level audit

Exact byte-level lineage — verified, not inferred from names

D2.8 (44,090 dots) is the common parent. On the 100 m UTM 11N grid:

  • GEMSDOE31 B1 = D2.8 minus 3,891 dots within 1 px (100 m) of the public catalogue fault raster → 40,199 dots. Verified: B1 is exactly D2.8 filtered at ≤100 m. [forensics JSON]
  • GEMSDOE32 B2 = B1 minus 2,545 dots at 100–200 m from the catalogue → 37,654 dots. Verified: B2 is exactly D2.8 filtered at ≤200 m; total deletions 6,436. [nine-site audit]
  • GEMSDOE36 = B2 plus 1,200 additions → 38,854 dots. Contains every B2 pixel.
  • GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 D2.8 champions are byte-identical decoded predictions despite different TIFF encodings — not unique.

Thus the nine “sites” are a single ridge-thinning lineage with progressive catalogue-distance pruning, not nine independent geological models. Copying any one violates the unique-submission rule.

Metric mechanism — plausible, not proven from hidden labels

The official metric is Tversky with triangular distance weighting: DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) where w(d)= max(1−d/300 m, 0).

  • Known-catalogue masking is not a blanket buffer: the evaluation description masks mapped catalogue pixels; it does not establish that every nearby pixel is false or receives zero hidden-truth credit. Any distance-based pruning therefore remains a model choice, not an organizer rule.
  • Plausible precision-cleanup mechanism, not a causal finding: removing predictions that contribute little hidden-truth coverage but still add weighted false-positive cost could improve DTI. We do not know the removed dots' hidden TPw or FPw contributions, so the 0.2600-to-0.2778 owner-reported difference cannot be decomposed or reproduced from public labels.
  • No identified score ceiling: the reported scalar score does not reveal private FNw, or imply that further pruning asymptotes at a particular value. The 200 m deletion boundary describes this raster's lineage, not a scientifically established optimum.
  • Addition comparisons are not attribution: GEMSDOE36 and GEMSDOE28-Rung30 have lower supplied claims than selected comparison files, but the unavailable hidden labels and unverified score receipts prevent concluding that additions specifically increased FPw faster than TPw.

Honest caveat: Hidden organizer labels and per-file submission receipts are unavailable; no one can prove causation from the reported number alone. The lineage and metric arithmetic are verified; the “why it scored” is the most parsimonious metric-consistent hypothesis, not a recovered ground-truth fact.

Can a genuinely unique submission beat 0.2778?

  • In principle, yes. The official public leaderboard observed 0.4544 on 27 Oct 2026 (xiaofanhu, rank-1) — far above 0.2778 — proving the task ceiling is not 0.28. But that is not a score for any file in this repository; we do not re-label public leaderboard observations as project-file receipts.
  • What a credible attempt needs: B2’s measured pixel lineage is subtractive, but the hidden TPw/FPw/FNw contributions are unknown. The owner-reported leaderboard values do not identify the additional recall needed for any target score. A new method would need independently validated evidence for unmapped fault structures; neither a numerical score gain nor a particular geophysical signature is established here.
  • Our measured ceiling so far: WBG-3 SPI-PMR-WCS-1 — the best same-evaluator, whole-component holdout result in this repo — achieves HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1182 (seg) / 0.1219 (intra-basin) with 4/4 fold wins over the 19-band control (+30.2%). It beats the control but trails the 10 m 3DEP benchmark S3DEP1 (0.16606) and hits the literal lattice STOP. No current research file beats the 0.2778 historical claim on a comparable instrument.
  • The literal registry blocker: The r13 spacing-5 synthetic lattice (206,895 dots) covers 99.8713% of the footprint within 3 px (300 m) [certificate], so by the pigeonhole principle any 36,800-dot dotted submission must have ≥81.932% forward overlap with it — exceeding the 70% literal threshold. No dotted file can clear literal uniqueness today without an updated registry policy that separates synthetic calibration lattices from competing method lanes. WBG-3 passes the competing-sparse gate (59.38% ≤70%; 27.55% vs B2) but correctly reports literal STOP and remains DO NOT SUBMIT.

Bottom line: GEMSDOE32 B2 is the clear historical leader among the nine focus sites for learning, but do not copy it. A truly winning unique submission needs a validated geophysical discovery model (see four hypotheses below), not a re-encoding of D2.8, and a registry fix before any weekly slot is spent.

A valid range alone does not satisfy scientific, uniqueness, or selector gates.
  3. At the official form: choose the single-band GeoTIFF or ZIP containing exactly one TIFF, enter the short approved note, check current weekly allowance in the logged-in account, then preserve the submission-page receipt.   4. If “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” appears: stop and validate the exact uploaded bytes; inspect finite/range counts and the reference grid. Do not silently clip or repair the file. Regenerate from the source using a documented transform, validate the exact final TIFF/ZIP, and only proceed if it is independently approved.
