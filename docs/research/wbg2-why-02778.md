# GEMSDOE32 B2: what the recorded pixels explain—and what they do not

**Status reviewed 2026-10-27.** The supplied GEMSDOE32 B2 value of **0.2778 is USER-REPORTED / NOT ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED**: no submission-page receipt tying that exact TIFF to the result is present in this repository. The official problem description and metric link below are retained for manual review; those external pages were not freshly retrieved in this review.

## Executive answer

Among the supplied focus-site results, GEMSDOE32 B2 has the highest reported value. The strongest defensible explanation is an **observed pixel-set change**, not a proven causal account of its score: B2 is a strict deletion-only subset of an older D2.8 field. It removes 6,436 predictions, adds none, and every removed prediction lies within 2 pixels (200 m on the recorded grid) of the mapped catalogue. The resulting file has 37,654 positive cells.

A distance-weighted max-cover metric can reward removing predictions whose marginal hidden-fault coverage is small while their false-positive contribution remains. That mechanism is consistent with the reported ordering. The hidden labels and an artifact-linked organizer receipt are absent, so it is **not proven** that every removed pixel was a false positive, that the deletion caused the reported increase, or that a 200 m buffer is generally optimal.

Whether a genuinely unique file can beat the supplied 0.2778 claim is **unknown**. WBG-2 does not have a valid spatial HOLDOUT-DTI, a receipt-backed calibration, or a defensible leaderboard estimate. Its old regression projection has been withdrawn and must not be quoted as a score.

## Why pruning could improve a max-cover score

The shared evaluator is the pinned implementation under `vendor/gems57/metric.py`; its source identity is recorded in `evidence/template_integrity.json`. At a high level, distance-weighted max-cover metrics reward predictions that cover withheld fault cells, while emitted predictions can also contribute false-positive cost. A new prediction may add little true-positive coverage if its neighborhood is already covered. Removing such a low-marginal-coverage prediction can therefore improve the ratio.

The catalogue-near location of the removed cells is a plausible proxy for low hidden-target value, but it is not proof. Some unrecorded faults could be close to mapped faults, and a static historical raster cannot be treated as a leak-free holdout prediction. A clean test would require the shared whole-segment hide-and-recover workflow, deriving catalogue features only from visible faults, pixel-exact masking, pooled DTI with the required kernel, and a paired confidence interval. **That test was not run for WBG-2.**

## What was withdrawn from the former WBG-2 page

The prior page included a nine-row regression projection, synthetic-trace comparisons, a reported spatially blocked KDE result, and metrics for an external detector. The committed evidence bundle did not contain the required reproducible WBG-2 holdout card or complete records supporting those claims. The regression also used unverified user-reported values, included duplicate prediction fields, had collinear predictors, and extrapolated outside its fit domain. Its values are now retained only as a quarantined historical projection in `registry/wbg2_calibration.json`; they are not a score estimator. The unsupported synthetic and detector performance claims have been removed from the current executive summary rather than repeated as findings.

The recorded `PROXY-DTI` of 0.0 against the mapped catalogue is by construction after the catalogue exclusion. It is not HOLDOUT-DTI and provides no evidence of recovery of hidden faults.

## WBG-2 artifact decision

The existing `docs/downloads/wbg2-offcatalogue-density-36k-20261027.tif` and one-member ZIP remain available **for research download only**; their bytes were preserved during this decision correction.
