Mathematical mechanism under pooled Distance-Weighted Tversky (α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m)

Under the competition metric, known catalogue pixels C are masked pixel-exactly (d=0), and each unmasked evaluation cell receives true-positive credit via a maximum-envelope triangular kernel K(d) = max(0, 1 - d / 300 m):

s(y) = max_{x in P} p(x) * max(0, 1 - ||y - x||_2 / 300 m)
DTI  = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 * FP_w + 0.8 * FN_w)

Differentiating DTI with respect to adding one unit binary dot (p(x) = 1) at current operating level DTI ≈ 0.278 shows that a dot improves the score if and only if its newly uncovered weighted truth credit exceeds a strict break-even threshold:

Delta(DTI) > 0   <==>   Delta(TP_w) / (1 - Delta(TP_w)_on_dot)  >  0.2 * DTI / (1 - 0.8 * DTI)  ≈  0.0715

Three structural geology & raster-geometry effects make 0 < d_cat(x) ≤ 200 m a severe negative-EV trap for ridge detectors trained on buffered catalogue traces:

  1. 1:100,000 digitization-halo & range-front scarp offset: Public USGS/INGENIOUS fault polylines digitized at 1:24k–1:100k carry 50–180 m planimetric offset relative to the sharpest 1 m LiDAR / 10 m 3DEP break-in-slope. A classifier trained on dilated catalogue buffers fires strongest at d_cat = 100–200 m along already-mapped faults where no unmapped fault exists, paying full 0.2 * (1 - 0) = 0.20 false-positive cost for zero TP_w.
  2. 300 m kernel overlap saturation across parallel splays: Even where a genuine secondary splay parallels a mapped master fault at 150 m, dots at d_cat > 200 m or along-strike extensions already cover up to 1 - 150/300 = 0.50 of its kernel envelope; under the max operator, redundant interior dots yield near-zero marginal Delta(TP_w).
  3. Exact FP savings from D2.8 → B2: Removing 6,436 halo dots cuts 0.2 * FP_w by up to 0.2 × 6,436 = 1,287.2 denominator units (509.0 units from B1 → B2 alone), lifting DTI without losing basin-interior blind fault coverage. Conversely, GEMSDOE36's +1,200 dots added ~240 denominator units without clearing the 0.0715 marginal TP_w bar per dot.
