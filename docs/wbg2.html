02 / WHY 0.2778 WON

Not geology. Catalogue distance and budget.

The mechanism is visible in the artifacts themselves.

THE MECHANISM

The metric pays you to delete bad guesses.

With α = 0.2 and β = 0.8, an off-target dot costs 0.2 while a dot exactly on a hidden fault earns 1.0 and removes a 0.8 false-negative penalty. Deleting a dot that sits on a mapped fault is therefore pure profit: the mapped fault is not a target.

That is why the highest-scoring focus-site artifact is the one with the fewest catalogue-adjacent dots — and why every one of the nine is a pruning lineage of the same base field rather than nine independent discoveries.

03 / CAN WE BEAT IT?

Yes in principle. Not with the data in this sandbox.

Three levers were tested numerically against the official metric.

LEVER 1 · PROVEN

Exclude the catalogue

A dot within 300 m of a mapped fault is close to worthless. WBG-2 excludes everything within 400 m — stricter than any of the nine (their best was 200 m).

Evidence: controlled deletion in GEMSDOE32's own record (+0.0070) and perfect rank order across nine files.

LEVER 2 · NEGATIVE

Continuous traces only help if you know the trace

Simulated against the official metric: a 1 px continuous trace along the true local strike scores 0.936; scattered dots score 0.140; runs at a guessed strike score 0.090 — worse than scattering.

Conclusion: WBG-2 scatters. Rendering is not the lever; knowing where the fault runs is.

LEVER 3 · UNRESOLVED

Shrink the field

At a fixed number of true hits, DTI rises monotonically as predicted pixels fall (0.132 → 0.221 going from 20,000 to 2,500 pixels). But shrinking only helps if the retained pixels are better than the discarded ones.

The nine-site data cannot separate budget from catalogue proximity (r = 0.75). WBG-2 therefore stays inside the observed budget range.

04 / NEGATIVE RESULTS

What we tried and threw away.

Both are recorded so the next session does not repeat them.

Official GeoDAWN + 3DEP detectorrejected

A fault-likeness score was built from official USGS GeoDAWN radiometrics (K, Th, U, TC; DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ) and USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM terrain descriptors instead of topography, gated by scarp asymmetry and structure-tensor line-likeness.

  • ×
    No discriminationAUC vs mapped faults: step 0.574, crest 0.585, asymmetry 0.493, line-likeness 0.497, radiometric alteration 0.487, combined 0.549.
  • ×
    Worse than random at the real budgetRanking the whole grid and taking 37,654 cells scored 0.0303 against the catalogue; a uniform random draw of the same size scored 0.0695.

Cause: at 100 m the shipped 8-bit rad/3DEP aggregates do not localise Great Basin faults. The official 19-band training_features.tif is the missing input and is login-gated.

WHAT THIS MEANS

The binding constraint is data, not cleverness.

Every route that could beat 0.28 requires a better fault-probability map. With only the catalogue plus weak external aggregates, the best available placement skill is roughly 2× chance — measured on a spatially blocked holdout — which is not enough to move a score meaningfully.

To reach the 0.39–0.45 leaderboard band, download the official training_features.tif, train the reference U-Net, then apply the three measured rules: exclude the catalogue, keep the field small, and never ship NaN.

07 / IRREGULARITIES

Flagged for review.

Things a reviewer should know before trusting any number on this page.

All nine reported scores are unverified third-party values

No submission-page receipt in this repository binds any of the nine artifacts to a score, and none binds WBG-2 to anything. The public leaderboard was observed at 0.4544 for rank 1 on 2026-10-27 (xiaofanhu), which supersedes the 0.4437 and 0.4413 figures quoted in earlier briefs. That observation says nothing about any file in this repository.

GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 publish the same predictions

Their files differ in bytes and encoding, but their 44,090 positive pixels are the same set, and both are reported at 0.2600. Treat the "nine sites" as at most eight independent fields.

The reported gap to the leaderboard is unexplained by the artifacts

The focus sites cluster at 0.245–0.278 while the public leaderboard runs 0.32–0.45. Nothing in the nine artifacts explains the gap; the most likely cause is access to the official 19-band feature stack, but that is an inference, not a measurement.

External layers used in the rejected experiment are peer re-distributions

The GeoDAWN radiometric and 3DEP terrain rasters were read from a peer repository, not from the primary USGS archive. Their sidecars record DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ and ScienceBase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7, but the bytes were not re-downloaded from USGS in this session. They are not used by WBG-2.

WBG-2 is a spatial prior, not a detection

WBG-2 places dots where mapped faults are dense, displaced away from the catalogue. It encodes no measurement of any individual fault. Its named non-fault mimic is basin geometry: basin-fill depocentres and range-front aprons concentrate near mapped faults, so the prior partly tracks sedimentary basins rather than faulting.
