## What the official evidence does and does not say

- [USGS Northern Granite Springs Valley characterization](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/a-geophysical-characterization-structure-and-geology-northern-granite-springs-valley) reports using gravity and magnetic derivatives for buried faults **and contacts**, including a possible dike swarm. This supports considering potential fields but also names an explicit non-fault alternative; it does not validate either proposed operator.
- [USGS Siler 2023](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/structural-discontinuities-and-their-control-hydrothermal-systems-great-basin-usa) discusses permeability-associated fault interactions; this does not establish that every fault is an active geothermal conduit.
- [USGS heat-flow release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/heat-flow-maps-and-supporting-data-great-basin-usa) says its maps estimate **background conductive** heat flow after de-emphasizing convective outliers; the well residual, not the background map by itself, is the relevant fluid-influence hypothesis.
- [DOE GDR INGENIOUS](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) provides the regional compilation under CC BY 4.0 and says it includes shallow temperatures, geophysics and chemistry. The page is available, not proof that an uninspected raster is aligned or free of catalogue leakage.
- The [official problem](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) measures new faults; the [USGS blind-system study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/discovering-blind-geothermal-systems-great-basin-region-integrated-geologic-and) used multiple independent data types and drilling to evaluate actual resources.
