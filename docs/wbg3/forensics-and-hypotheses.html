EVIDENCE REVIEW / GEMSDOE32 B2

What the B2 raster ancestry shows — and what it cannot prove about reported 0.2778

The score is OWNER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED. Exact pixel ancestry is reproducible; causal attribution is not. No hidden labels or artifact-linked organizer receipt are available.

Verified raster factsMeasured from the inspected GeoTIFFs
  • The D2.8 parent contains 44,090 positive pixels; B1 contains 40,199; GEMSDOE32 B2 contains 37,654.
  • B1 is the exact parent with 3,891 pixels removed within one 100 m pixel of the public catalogue. B2 removes 2,545 further pixels at distances greater than one and no more than two pixels. Overall, B2 removes 6,436 parent pixels, all within 200 m, and adds none.
  • This establishes a deletion-only ancestry, not what hidden labels assigned to the removed pixels. “No new detections” describes the raster set relation; it does not explain the reported score change.

Exact source hashes and the qualified interpretation are recorded in evidence/h33_b2_forensics.json. A 200 m buffer is not an organizer rule or a universal geological exclusion.

Metric-level conditional, not a causal findingOfficial metric reference linked below

The competition uses a distance-weighted Tversky metric with a 300 m triangular kernel and weights α = 0.2, β = 0.8:

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 × FP_w + 0.8 × FN_w)

If some removed pixels receive little or no hidden-truth credit while contributing false-positive cost, pruning them could improve DTI. But omitted hidden faults may also lie near mapped faults. Without the hidden truth, the actual TPw, FPw, FNw and evaluated upload are unknown, so this conditional cannot be promoted to the cause of the owner-reported result.

The arithmetic difference between the two supplied owner reports is 0.0178; it is not an organizer-confirmed paired score delta.

Previously measured WBG-3 package and ranked prospective hypotheses

The existing SPI-PMR-WCS package has a historical HOLDOUT-DTI of 0.118180 (95% CI [0.103658, 0.132432], evaluator gems-segment-pooled-v1, 60,988 withheld positives). This is prior package-level evidence—not a new test of the hypotheses below, not evidence that any individual physical mechanism caused the result, and not a leaderboard score. Its final-dot audit has a literal STOP; the verdict remains negative.

The four proposals below are qualitative candidates from the ranked hypothesis note. Rank 1 is the proposed top candidate by qualitative benefit-to-cost, not by a score projection. All four exact formulations remain untested in this repository.

  1. Rank 1 — multi-scale magnetic-texture autocorrelation relative to basement contours. Use cached rtp, tmi, and literal depth_to_base_surf channels to compare texture autocorrelation parallel and perpendicular to local band-15 contours across several lags. Hypothesis: a covered displacement may interrupt coherent magnetic basement fabric where a surface trace is absent. Named mimics: lithologic contacts, dike swarms, and flight-line leveling. Status: not run; channel physical semantics and organizer-origin provenance are not independently authenticated.
  2. Rank 2 — radiometric-contact and terrain-break concordance. Compare co-located K / eTh / eU radiometric gradients with DEM slope or curvature breaks across scales; penalize isolated single-sensor edges. Hypothesis: shallow alteration or structural juxtaposition may produce coincident signals. Named mimics: lithology, soil moisture, vegetation, and acquisition artifacts. The USGS GeoDAWN page is linked for review; raw survey coverage was not rechecked here.
  3. Rank 3 — gravity-residual lineament persistence. If the official cached stack contains the required gravity band, test directional curvature or second-derivative ridges persisting across physically plausible wavelengths. Hypothesis: density contrasts may reveal buried basin structures not expressed as surface scarps. Named mimics: basin margins and lithologic boundaries. Band availability, provenance, and a feature-only leakage screen must be established before any validation.
  4. Rank 4 — drainage or valley deflection at fault crossings. From a verified DEM, test repeated channel deflections supported by local relief and curvature rather than raw lineament density. Hypothesis: displaced drainage may expose a geomorphic expression missing from a fault catalogue. Named mimics: lithologic control, engineered drainage, landslides, and glacial terrain. No new external data source or coverage is assumed.

Top-candidate validation decision: no validation run was authorized or performed. No assigned lane-method paragraph was found, and the relevant scientific lane is stopped. The existing SPI-PMR-WCS HOLDOUT-DTI must not be relabeled as a result for Rank 1. Do not start an experiment or use a submission slot until a valid lane and authorization exist.
