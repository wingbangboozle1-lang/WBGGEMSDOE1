Hypotheses, and what happened to each

A hypothesis only enters a submission after it has been scored on withheld truth with the official metric. Five were tested this lane; one survived.

Ranked by expected DTI gain per unit of implementation cost

  1. Tip propagation (adopted). A mapped fault continues along its own strike beyond the last mapped pixel. Cost: trivial. Gain: the largest of anything tested — holdout 0.3816 versus 0.2623 for the dotted shape, and 3.5× the ring-uniform null.
  2. Grain-aligned dashes (rejected). Emitting short dashes along the local strike field instead of single points raised line coherence from 0.08 to 0.45 but scored below the plain trace: the extra pixels on the dash are pixels the metric charges for.
  3. Relay and step-over bridging (rejected). Connecting quasi-collinear tips 4–30 px apart is geologically sound and scored 0.3558 against 0.3816 for the unbridged trace.
  4. Lidar-scarp gating (rejected, with a number). Restricting extensions to tips that sit on a 3DEP scarp cost 0.14 of holdout DTI, because the scarp composite only reaches AUC 0.575 on this region and lidar covers three quarters of the footprint.
  5. A supervised 19-band detector (rejected). A HistGradientBoosting model over the official 19-band stack plus 56 derivative channels, trained in four geographically blocked folds, reaches AUC 0.63–0.71 on held-out blocks — real but weak skill — and emits a field that scores 0.05 on the withheld-tail truth. It finds faults that are already mapped.

What would actually raise the ceiling

  • Per-tip continuation length. Fifteen pixels is one number for 4 594 tips. The distribution of segment lengths in the catalogue is heavy-tailed; a tip on a 40-px segment should probably continue further than a tip on a 9-px one. Cheap to test, unimplemented.
  • Tip-quality priors. Not every tip deserves a continuation. A tip whose extension runs into another fault at a high angle is a step-over, not a continuation — the geometry to detect that is already computed in structure.py.
  • Off-catalogue truth. Every holdout in this repository is built from the catalogue, so all of them reward re-finding mapped geometry. The only artefact that could settle the real question is a public, expert-mapped, off-catalogue fault set for this region; the SGMC normal-fault layer used by the sibling repositories is the nearest thing and it is a different scale of mapping.
