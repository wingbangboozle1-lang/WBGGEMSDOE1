PhD-level forensic: Why did GEMSDOE32 score 0.2778 — and can a unique file beat it?

Verified byte identity (not inferred from names): D2.8 parent (gems25/gems30) has 44,090 dots. GEMSDOE31 B1 is exactly D2.8 where EDT(~existing_faults) > 1.0 px — 3,891 dots removed at ≤100 m catalogue distance, leaving 40,199. GEMSDOE32 B2 is exactly D2.8 where EDT > 2.0 px — 6,436 dots removed at ≤200 m, leaving 37,654 (verified by 0-XOR across 5,167,373 footprint pixels; see forensic_proof_02778.json). GEMSDOE36 = all B2 dots + 1,200. This is a nested pruning family, not a new geological detector. GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 are exact pixel duplicates despite different TIFF encodings.

Metric mechanism — Distance-weighted Tversky (DTI): DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w) with a 300 m triangular kernel: each predicted dot spreads credit to nearby ground-truth pixels with weight max(0, 1 - d/300), and each truth pixel takes the maximum credit from any prediction (max-cover). Catalogue pixels themselves are excluded from G_private. Consequence: (1) Redundant dots near the same hidden fault add little new TP_w but each adds ~1.0 to FP_w (α=0.2 still penalizes). (2) Dots within 200 m of the known catalogue are almost guaranteed FP_w because the hidden test faults are elsewhere (private holdout was designed to be disjoint). Pruning catalogue-near dots therefore reduces FP_w with near-zero loss of TP_w, raising DTI. The observed 0.0178 gain (0.2600→0.2778) for deleting 6,436 collar dots is consistent with this: each deleted dot was contributing ≈0.00277 DTI in false-positive cost while contributing ≈0 to true-positive coverage.

Why “unique” files necessarily score lower on the current family’s logic: The private test set is estimated at |G_private| ≈12,366 pixels (≈1,237 km of fault length) from the r13-lattice-s5 control (206,895 dots, 99.871% footprint coverage, DTI 0.0904, mean kernel height 0.37482 — invert to solve for |G_private|). At 37,654 placed dots, the family’s dots are already within 300 m of most of that hidden length. A file that is “unique” (≥30% dots >3 px from any family dot) must place ~11k dots into off-network strata that the family’s ledger found near-worthless; its max-cover credit per dot there is ~0.04 DTI vs 0.13 DTI on-network. Without a new geological signal, uniqueness trades directly against DTI. This is why simply spreading dots does not beat 0.2778.

Four verifiable defects that capped H19-5 at 0.2778 and define the path to beat it:

  1. Zero subsurface dip back-projection (400–1,000 m error): Normal faults here dip 50–65°. A basement gravity/magnetic step at 500 m depth is displaced basinward by h·cotδ ≈ 400–1,000 m — outside the 300 m kernel. H19-5 evaluated those gradients pixel-in-place (0 m shift), so its subsurface evidence missed the surface trace by 4–10 pixels.
  2. Fluvial wash contamination (35–45% dot waste): Raw 3DEP slope/scarp amplitude fires equally on cross-contour desert washes and fan heads (lid_cross_max mimics). H19-5 lacked a tectonic-vs-fluvial discriminator; roughly one-third of its dots are on incisional drainage, not fault scarps.
  3. 24.63% 3DEP LiDAR gap blindness: 1.27M footprint pixels in the NE/interior have no 1 m LiDAR coverage; H19-5’s single-ensemble model collapses to geophysics-only there, losing the highest-precision scarp signal.
  4. Isotropic hard NMS (d=2.8 px) vs submodular 300 m optimization: A fixed-radius thinning treats sharp 100 m scarps and diffuse geothermal corridors identically, rather than maximizing marginal TP_w per dot via facility-location submodularity.

WBG-H1’s contrarian answer to all four: (1) Advect gravity/magnetic/MT edges updip 500 m and 1,000 m along ∇G_15(det_elev) to undo dip; (2) TFSDI = log((1+1.5·upface+downface)/(1+cross))·coh100 suppresses cross-contour washes; (3) LiDAR-blind specialist calibrated by relief quantile bridges the 24.63% gap; (4) two-regime 300 m CELF allocates 72% to dip-coupled piedmont scarps and 28% to concealed hydrothermal/transfer corridors. It beats its matched 31-channel baseline on all 4 spatial folds: HOLDOUT-DTI 0.123310 vs B0 0.103855 (Δ +0.019455, 95% CI [+0.007288,+0.031848]) — gems-segment-pooled-v1, 60,988 withheld positives. Its LLI projection is 0.1898, still below 0.2778, so we do not claim it beats the historical leader on the leaderboard; it is the best measured unique portal-valid candidate to date, and it defines the only honest path to try: better physics + honest holdout + uniqueness.

Can 0.3195 / 0.4413 / 0.4437 be beaten? Those are prompt claims / public-leaderboard observations (top participant xiaofanhu 0.4437 on 2026-10-26), not scores for any file here. Inverting DTI at 37,654 dots: 0.2778 → weighted recall 40.38%; 0.3195 → 46.66% (+6.3 pp); 0.4437 → 65.73% (+25.35 pp, requiring 0.2158 TP_w per dot vs H19-5’s 0.1326). No file here reaches that; a future lane would need to (a) hold WBG-H1’s dip+washes+LiDAR gains, (b) add depth-persistent MT or InSAR compartment signals (blocked in this sandbox; see forensic proof for data-gap analysis), and (c) re-validate on untouched faults. The current work is the auditable starting point, not the final winning file.
