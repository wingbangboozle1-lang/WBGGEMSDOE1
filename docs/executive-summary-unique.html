2. Why GEMSDOE32 B2 scored 0.2778, and whether we can beat it

Short answer: GEMSDOE32 B2 is the inherited D2.8 field (44,090 dots) pruned twice: first removing dots within 100 m of the public catalogue (→ B1, 40,199 dots), then removing dots within 200 m (→ B2, 37,654 dots). Its reported 0.2778 is USER-REPORTED, not organizer-confirmed. No submission-page receipt binds those bytes to that value.

Plausible mechanism: the official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index (α = 0.2 on false positives, β = 0.8 on false negatives, 300 m triangular kernel). Under max-cover credit, each truth cell receives at most the single highest-confidence prediction within 300 m. Redundant dots near already-covered faults add false-positive mass without new coverage. Pruning low-marginal-credit dots (those near the catalogue) can therefore raise DTI — but this is a plausible explanation, not a proved causal account, because hidden labels are unavailable.

Can it be beaten? In principle yes — the observed official public leaderboard shows higher values (0.4544 was observed on 2026-10-27; the current target to beat is ≈0.32–0.45). But no experiment in this session establishes a higher project leaderboard result. Our generated file has HOLDOUT-DTI 0.4657, but that value may be inflated by leakage (full-catalogue dot placement). A defensible replacement must beat a clean, identically evaluated current-best benchmark, pass final-dot novelty after surface clearance, and survive untouched confirmation on independent data.

Physical signature: blind faults occur adjacent to — not on — mapped traces (splays, stepovers, blind extensions). Smooth Gaussian distance kernel peaking ~150 m from catalogue, decaying toward both the catalogue (already mapped) and far background.

Off-catalogue rationale: targets faults near but not on mapped traces — precisely the faults the catalogue misses. GEMSDOE32 B2 used a hard >200 m cut; this uses a smooth peak at ~150 m.
