“Historical claim” is deliberately not labelled ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED: there is no submission-page receipt binding those exact bytes to the supplied values. No defensible leaderboard projection is available. A different pixel set is not equivalent to passing the literal lane gate.

Why did the GEMSDOE32 file lead the supplied group?

What the bytes establish: D2.8 has 44,090 dots. The GEMSDOE31 B1 field removes 3,891 dots at catalogue distance ≤100 m, leaving 40,199. GEMSDOE32 B2 removes another 2,545 at distance ≤200 m, leaving 37,654. These are exact set identities on the recovered grid. GEMSDOE36 preserves all of B2 and adds 1,200 dots.

The metric mechanism is plausible, not proven from hidden labels: distance-weighted true-positive credit saturates at the maximum contributing confidence for each truth cell. Redundant predictions can add false-positive mass with little new coverage. Sparse placement and pruning low-marginal-credit dots can therefore improve DTI. The supplied historical values are consistent with that explanation, but they do not prove where hidden faults were or establish a universal 200 m exclusion rule.

Can it be beaten? In principle yes: the directly observed official public leaderboard is higher. But no experiment here establishes a higher project leaderboard result, and this negative screen is not a contender. A defensible replacement must beat a clean, identically evaluated current best, pass final-dot novelty after surface clearance, and survive untouched confirmation.
