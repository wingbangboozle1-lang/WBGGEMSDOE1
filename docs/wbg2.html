Why GEMSDOE32's B2 might have performed well

In the prior pixel audit, D2.8 had 44,090 dots, B1 40,199, and B2 37,654 after removing dots close to the mapped catalogue. GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have the same decoded dot field; GEMSDOE36 contains B2 and 1,200 additions. These related fields are not nine independent discoveries.

The organizer's 300 m triangular kernel gives diminishing distance credit, and α=0.2 reduces the penalty for false positives while β=0.8 penalizes missed positives. Removing redundant predictions could improve the metric if they contributed little hidden-fault coverage. The hidden labels and a file-specific receipt are unavailable, so neither the exact reason for the reported 0.2778 nor an improvement beyond it is established. A 200 m or 400 m exclusion is not an organizer rule; the WBG-2 buffer was a four-step Manhattan mask, not a true 400 m Euclidean disc.

Could a genuinely unique file beat 0.2778? In principle, yes. The official public leaderboard recorded 0.4544 (2026-10-27). This exceeds the supplied historical value, but does not demonstrate that any candidate in this repository is both unique under the stated registry rule and better; WBG-2 is not such a candidate. A credible answer requires a properly gated and measured prospective test.

Fault likelihood is not proof of geothermal vents, permeability or economically recoverable resources. See USGS blind-system research and the official problem and metric.

What must happen before a future upload

  1. Assign one prospective method lane and address the literal saturated-registry obstruction centrally; do not optimize around its uncovered holes.
  2. Run feature canaries and visible-only, buffered whole-segment holdout. Beat a reproducible same-evaluator current-best baseline with appropriate paired uncertainty.
  3. Pass both pre-placement and final-dot registry checks, exact TIFF/ZIP checks, and a separate selector with current account allowance. Only then use the official submission form and retain its receipt.
