Why the 0.2778 claim is not a score forecast

The local raster audit shows that B2 is a 37,654-dot subset of a 44,090-dot D2.8 parent. If the owner-reported values are accurate, the sparser B2 field scored 0.0178 higher than D2.8. A plausible explanation is that removing predictions with little marginal kernel credit reduces false-positive cost while preserving coverage. That relation does not reveal hidden labels, prove which removed dots were false positives, authenticate the score, or justify applying the same buffer to a new field.

There is no defensible estimate for a new TIFF. The historical `0.2778`, conflicting user-reported leaderboard claims `0.4413` and `0.3195`, and dated public leaderboard observation `0.4544` are different evidence classes. The competition page was not freshly reachable from this sandbox; none of these figures is a project file's confirmed score.

Ranked next hypotheses — proposals, not experiments

Qualitative potential and implementation cost only; no numeric DTI is projected. Exact operators are not recorded as local experiments, but related components exist in prior local and peer work. The newest peer repository heads are not content-audited here, so global novelty is unresolved.

1 · Multiscale magnetic-texture anisotropy (MTX1) — medium potential, medium cost

Use literal cached bands 2 (`rtp`), 14 (`tmi`), optionally 15 (`depth_to_base_surf`): compare local autocorrelation/structure-tensor anisotropy parallel and normal to band-15 contours at fixed 300/800 m scales. A buried discontinuity could interrupt magnetic fabric beyond a mapped scarp. Mimics include dikes, plutons, remanence changes, and survey leveling. The cache exists and hashes match an owner mirror, but semantics and organizer provenance remain unresolved. Not tested: lane assignment is missing and the active lane is stopped.

2 · Cross-scale gravity–magnetic–relief edge displacement (XGRAD1) — low–moderate potential, medium cost

Use cached bands 13, 14, and 12 to track relative orientation and offset of gradient maxima across fixed scales. A concealed contact may remain in potential fields while relief is weak. Mimics include intrusive contacts, basin margins, and survey seams. Similar multisensor ingredients occur in prior parallel work, so incremental novelty is limited.

3 · Radiometric compositional closure across terrain saddles (RDX1) — low–moderate potential, medium–high cost

Use GeoDAWN K/Th/U and `det_elev` for local log-ratio closure residuals across saddles. Alteration is one possible source; sediment sorting, moisture, attenuation, and lithology can mimic it. Official source: USGS GeoDAWN, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ. The source page was in the dated audit; raw products were not fetched here, so this is not ready to validate.

4 · USGS well-residual heat-flow discontinuity (HFW1) — uncertain, high data/QA cost

Test measured well residuals after background conductive trend with spatial sampling controls and cached potential-field context. Groundwater, thermal-conductivity error, elevation, and clustered drilling are mimics. Official free/CC0 source: USGS Great Basin heat flow, DOI 10.5066/P9BZPVUC. Raw files and coverage were not obtained in this sandbox; not viable for validation yet.

5 · INGENIOUS shallow temperature-probe residuals (IGT1) — uncertain, high QA cost

Cross-fit elevation/season corrections to 2 m probe observations and test their relation to structural context. Moisture, vegetation, aspect, season, lithology, and sampling bias are mimics. Source: DOE GDR INGENIOUS 1391 (DOI 10.15121/1881483; CC BY 4.0 in the dated source audit). Archive/coverage were not verified in this environment; not viable yet.
