2. Why did GEMSDOE32 B2 score 0.2778, and can it be beaten?

Short answer: GEMSDOE32 B2 is the inherited D2.8 dot field pruned by catalogue distance: D2.8 (44,090 dots, shared with GEMSDOE30) → GEMSDOE31 B1 removes dots within 100 m of the public catalogue (40,199 dots) → GEMSDOE32 B2 removes another 2,545 dots within 200 m of the catalogue (37,654 dots). The reported 0.2778 is USER-REPORTED, not organizer-confirmed; no submission-page receipt binds those bytes to that value.

Plausible mechanism (not proved): the official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index (α = 0.2 false positives, β = 0.8 false negatives, 300 m triangular kernel). Under max-cover credit, each truth cell earns credit from the single strongest prediction within 300 m. Redundant dots near already-covered faults add false-positive cost without new coverage, so pruning low-marginal-credit, catalogue-near dots can raise DTI. That makes 0.2778 plausible as a precision play — but hidden labels are unavailable, so this is a plausible mechanism, not a proved causal account, and a 200 m exclusion is not an organizer rule.

Can it be beaten? In principle yes — the public leaderboard shows higher values — but nothing in this repo establishes a higher project result, and we cannot verify any claim without DrivenData authentication. The new TIF above is genuine and unique; treat its leaderboard score as unknown. Do not copy GEMSDOE32 B2.
